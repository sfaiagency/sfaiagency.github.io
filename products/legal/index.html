Solutions

Legal

Courts are deciding how copyright law applies to AI. The evidence often sits inside huge training datasets and inside the models. Normal discovery often can’t reach it. Our tool, Croquis, can.

Assess: find out if a claim has facts behind it before you file, or screen a portfolio for claims worth bringing. Prove: build documented, repeatable evidence for the works at issue. See a sample report.

What you get

Assess

Check the facts before you file.

  • Check a client’s catalog, or a whole group of potential plaintiffs, against public training datasets. See whose work appears where.
  • Test the models for signs they learned specific works, from exact copies to close imitation.
  • Separate claims backed by evidence from claims based on suspicion.
  • Find patterns across many rights holders and works. These matter for class and group actions.

Prove

Turn a strong suspicion into evidence, work by work.

  • A report for each work: where it appears in training data, how the model responds when tested, and what that shows. Outputs attached.
  • Every test is logged and can be re-run, so others can check the results.
  • Based on our published research, so your experts can review and cite the methods.
  • We keep checking as new models come out, so the record covers ongoing use.

Where the evidence matters

Before filing

A factual basis

Claims in a complaint need support. Dataset and model analysis turns “we think our client’s work was used” into specific findings.

In discovery

Better requests

Knowing which datasets, model versions, and behaviors matter helps you ask for the right things, and check what the other side hands over.

At scale

Class and portfolio actions

When a case covers thousands of works, the analysis has to cover them too. Findings for each work show the common patterns.

We give technical analysis, not legal advice. The legal strategy stays yours.
