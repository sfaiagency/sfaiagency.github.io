About a year ago, my friend and fellow political cartoonist, [Kevin Kallaugher ("KAL")](https://kaltoons.substack.com/), was let go by the Baltimore Sun. KAL is an internationally renowned, award-winning editorial cartoonist.

Recently, the Baltimore Sun found a replacement. But that replacement wasn't another cartoonist. Instead, editors made the decision to use [cheap AI slop images](https://editorialcartoonists.com/an-open-letter-to-the-publisher-and-editors-of-the-baltimore-sun-from-the-aaec/). Images "produced using artificial intelligence tools by the Baltimore Sun editorial staff" began running in August.

A hedge fund bought the nearly 200-year-old Baltimore Sun in 2021 and the head of Sinclair Broadcast Group took over in 2024. Do the current owners think publishing AI slop is a good way to retain existing readers and bring in new, younger ones?

The one silver lining is that the editorial staff at the Sun have made it perfectly clear that AI is no match for a well thought out, well drawn [political cartoon drawn by a human being](https://www.kaltoons.com/portfolio/) like the award-winning work created by KAL and his peers.

Much of the existing AI technology that allows for the creation of the kind of machine-generated images that the Sun is publishing is only possible thanks to art created by actual humans. Unfortunately the artists behind those works are rarely asked for their permission, let alone compensated. Often they're unaware that their work was used at all.

I believe that human creators come first, deserve protection from AI theft and should be paid if their work is used to train AI models.

I am proud that KAL has signed on as one of SFAI's Early Adopters.

As AI innovation grows by leaps and bounds, maintaining human agency in these tools is what I strive to do at SFAI every day.

*(And when I have time after that, I still draw cartoons.)*

-Mark
