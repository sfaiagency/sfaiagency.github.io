There is something about cartoonists and their work that gives them the dubious distinction of being [canaries in a coal mine](https://en.wiktionary.org/wiki/canary_in_a_coal_mine#English). Whether it is their place in the brutal business of newspapers and journalism, amid the rise of ever-fickle social media algorithms or in today's era of ravenous AI, look to cartoonists for early social, technical and social trends. Trends that have a dramatic impact on the life and work of these specialized artists, and eventually, everyone else.

The latest thing? ChatGPT [signing real cartoonists' names](https://www.niemanlab.org/2026/10/chatgpt-is-adding-real-cartoonists-signatures-to-fake-new-yorker-cartoons/) to AI-generated work.

While that may not seem like a huge deal, it's basically stealing someone's identity. Would you want your name signed to an image, document or statement that you don't believe in? (To say nothing of being credited for poor artwork.)

This latest development comes after cartoonists' work has been used to train the very models producing cartoon slop now competing (for attention and dollars) with original cartoons created by actual humans.

Or, as an [OpenAI exec said](https://www.nytimes.com/2026/09/17/technology/microsoft-openai-publishing-industry.html?unlocked_article_code=1.GVE.-cqm.m3VAbz1sR6HS&smid=url-share), this "is going to increasingly lead to us creating systems that substitute for the labor of the people that define the 'culture' of society."
